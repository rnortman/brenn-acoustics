The echo canceller models the path from the reference to a microphone as linear. A small loudspeaker driven near its limit distorts, and a linear filter cannot remove what the distortion adds. A static non-linearity applied to the reference ahead of the cancellers, fitted from recordings of the loudspeaker, is the usual remedy and is not built: the first version of the algorithms stops at the linear canceller, and on the recordings it was planned from, made well below the loudspeaker's limit, a linear fit already reaches about the room's own level. Done when recordings at a playback level where the residual after the linear canceller stands measurably above the room show how much of it is the loudspeaker's distortion, and either a model ahead of the cancellers lowers it by a stated amount on held-out recordings, or the measurement shows there is nothing to model at the levels the device plays at and this entry is removed.
